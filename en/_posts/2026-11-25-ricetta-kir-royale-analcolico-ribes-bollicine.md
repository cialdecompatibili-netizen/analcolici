<p>The <strong>kir royale</strong> is the French toast par excellence: <strong>blackcurrant liqueur (crème de cassis) and bubbles</strong>. The <strong>non-alcoholic</strong> version uses blackcurrant syrup and 0% sparkling wine, giving a ruby-red, elegant drink ready in two minutes.</p>
<h2>Ingredients (1 flute)</h2>
<ul><li>15 ml blackcurrant syrup or cream</li><li>100 ml well-chilled 0% sparkling wine</li><li>Optional: a blackberry or raspberry to garnish</li></ul>
<h2>Method</h2>
<p>Pour the blackcurrant syrup into the bottom of a cold flute. Add the 0% sparkling slowly, tilting the glass, to keep the bubbles. Do not stir: the colour fades on its own from ruby to pink. Serve at once.</p>
<h2>Tips for a perfect result</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Ratio</strong>: 1 part syrup to 6-7 parts bubbles; more hides the aroma.</li><li><strong>Temperature</strong>: bubbles at 6-8°C and the flute kept in the freezer for 10 minutes.</li><li><strong>Variation</strong>: with raspberry instead of blackcurrant it becomes a pink kir royale.</li></ul>
<h2>What to pair it with</h2>
<p>It works with <strong>oysters</strong>, <strong>goat cheese</strong>, <strong>vegetable pâté</strong> and <strong>chocolate desserts</strong>. For more toast ideas see the <a href="/en/blog/mocktail-recipes/ricetta-bellini-analcolico-pesca-veneto/">non-alcoholic bellini</a>.</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>What is the difference between kir and kir royale?</h3><p>A kir uses white wine, a kir royale uses sparkling wine or champagne: here the base is always 0%.</p>
<h3>Can I use blackcurrant juice?</h3><p>Yes, but add a teaspoon of sugar: juice is more acidic than syrup.</p>
