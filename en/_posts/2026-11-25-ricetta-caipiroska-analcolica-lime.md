<p>The <strong>caipiroska</strong> is the cousin of the caipirinha: vodka replaces cachaça, but the technique is identical. In the <strong>non-alcoholic</strong> («virgin caipiroska») version you keep the muddled lime, cane sugar and lots of crushed ice, for a tangy, fresh and instant drink.</p>
<h2>Ingredients (1 short glass)</h2>
<ul><li>1 unwaxed lime, cut into pieces</li><li>2 teaspoons cane sugar</li><li>50 ml neutral 0% spirit</li><li>Crushed ice</li><li>Optional: 30 ml soda</li></ul>
<h2>Method</h2>
<p>Cut the lime into pieces, removing the white central pith, and put it in the glass with the sugar. <strong>Muddle gently</strong>: you want to release the juice, not crush the peel, which would make the drink bitter. Fill with crushed ice, pour the 0% spirit and stir from the bottom. Add soda if you want a longer drink.</p>
<h2>Tips</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Ice</strong>: crushed is best, it melts slowly and keeps cold.</li><li><strong>Sugar</strong>: cane sugar, which dissolves well in the juice.</li><li><strong>Too bitter?</strong> Remove the white core of the lime.</li></ul>
<h2>Fruit variations</h2>
<p>Add strawberries, kiwi or passion fruit muddled with the lime. If you prefer fresher herbal drinks, try the <a href="/en/blog/mocktail-recipes/ricetta-mojito-analcolico-menta-lime/">non-alcoholic mojito</a>.</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>What is the difference between caipiroska and caipirinha?</h3><p>The caipiroska uses vodka, the caipirinha cachaça: in the 0% version only the spirit's character changes.</p>
<h3>Can I use lemon instead of lime?</h3><p>Yes, but it will taste sharper: add a little sugar.</p>
