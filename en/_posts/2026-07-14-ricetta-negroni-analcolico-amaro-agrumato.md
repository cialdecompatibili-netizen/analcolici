<p>The Negroni is one of the most iconic cocktails and at the same time the most difficult to reproduce in a non-alcoholic version, because its identity is built entirely on bitterness: without gin and Campari, the challenge is not to lose that characteristic dryness that makes the Negroni recognizable among a thousand aperitifs. In recent years the market for non-alcoholic bitters and vermouths has grown enough to allow a faithful reconstruction, but even in their absence a convincing result can be obtained with a few precautions.</p><h2>Ingredients (1 small glass)</h2><ul><li>40 ml of bitter red non-alcoholic bitters (or pomegranate juice mixed with 2-3 drops of non-alcoholic angostura)</li><li>40 ml non-alcoholic red vermouth (or red grape juice with a pinch of rhubarb powder)</li><li>40 ml of slightly bitter non-alcoholic tonic soda</li><li>Ice in large cubes</li><li>An orange peel for garnish</li></ul><h2>Procedure</h2><p>Fill a short tumbler with ice, preferably large cubes that melt more slowly. Pour the non-alcoholic bitters, non-alcoholic vermouth and tonic soda directly onto the ice. Unlike many mocktails, the non-alcoholic Negroni should not be shaken but mixed: use a long spoon to gently stir the liquid for 10-15 seconds, so as to mix without diluting too much. Garnish by twisting a large orange peel over the glass to release the essential oils, then drop it in.</p><h2>Because it works</h2><p>The structure of the Negroni rests on three pillars of equal weight: bitter, sweet-herbaceous and sparkling-dry. Replacing each component with a non-alcoholic equivalent that maintains the same function, instead of simply diluting it with fruit juice, is what allows the complexity of the original to be preserved. The bitter red bitter is the most difficult element to replicate: if you can't find a ready-made non-alcoholic version, pure (unsweetened) pomegranate juice combined with a few drops of non-alcoholic angostura comes very close to the profile of Campari.</p><h2>A tip for balance</h2><p>Always taste before adding tonic soda: its bitterness varies greatly from brand to brand, and it may be necessary to reduce it slightly if the bitter you are using is already quite intense, to avoid an excessively bitter result.</p>
