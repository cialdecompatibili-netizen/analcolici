<p>The Cosmopolitan is probably the most photographed cocktail in recent history, made iconic by its intense pink color and the martini glass that accompanies it. Its structure, apparently simple, hides a delicate balance between acidity, sweetness and the citrus note that must remain the protagonist. The non-alcoholic version works exactly on this balance, using cranberry juice instead of Cointreau and vodka to obtain the same color and complexity in the mouth, without alcohol content.</p><h2>Ingredients (1 cocktail glass)</h2><ul><li>90 ml of unsweetened cranberry juice</li><li>30 ml fresh lime juice</li><li>15ml simple sugar syrup</li><li>15 ml of fresh orange juice</li><li>Plenty of ice for the shaker</li><li>A lime or orange zest for garnish</li></ul><h2>Procedure</h2><p>Fill a shaker three-quarters full with ice. Pour in the cranberry juice, lime juice, sugar syrup and orange juice. Close and shake vigorously for at least 15 seconds: it is the vigorous movement that creates the light surface foam typical of the Cosmopolitan served in bars. Filter through a sieve directly into a very cold martini glass, without ice. Garnish by twisting a lime or orange peel over the glass to release the essential oils, then place it on the rim.</p><h2>Because it works</h2><p>The key to this mocktail is the balance between the cranberry juice, naturally acidic and slightly bitter, and the controlled sweetness of the syrup: too much syrup flattens the taste, too little makes it unpleasantly acidic. The touch of orange juice adds a roundness that in the original recipe comes from Cointreau, maintaining that complex citrus note that makes the Cosmopolitan recognizable at the first sip.</p><h2>A bartender's tip</h2><p>Always chill the martini glass in the freezer for a few minutes before serving: a lukewarm Cosmopolitan loses much of its charm. If you don't have a shaker, you can get a good result by vigorously mixing the ingredients with ice in an airtight jar.</p>
