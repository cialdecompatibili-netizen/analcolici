<p><strong>Non-alcoholic gin</strong> is an alcohol-free botanical spirit: it keeps the <strong>juniper, citrus and spice</strong> profile of classic gin but contains no alcohol. It is the ideal base for a gin and tonic, a fizz or a martini without giving up the aroma.</p>
<h2>How it is made</h2>
<p>Producers distil or macerate botanicals (juniper, coriander, citrus peel) in water, then concentrate the aromas without adding final alcohol. The result is lighter-bodied than gin, but cleaner and more fragrant.</p>
<h2>How to choose a good non-alcoholic gin</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Recognisable juniper</strong>: without it, it is not a gin.</li><li><strong>Low sugar</strong>: read the label.</li><li><strong>Declared botanicals</strong>: a sign of serious production.</li></ul>

<h2>4 ways to use it</h2>
<ol><li><strong>Gin and tonic</strong>: 50 ml, premium tonic, cucumber.</li><li><strong>Gin fizz</strong>: with lemon, sugar and soda.</li><li><strong>Basil smash</strong>: muddled with basil and lime.</li><li><strong>Martini</strong>: with 0% dry vermouth and a lemon twist.</li></ol>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Does non-alcoholic gin taste like gin?</h3><p>It has the juniper aroma but lacks the warmth of alcohol: a good tonic helps bring it out.</p>
<h3>Does it keep like gin?</h3><p>Once opened, keep it in the fridge and use it within a few weeks.</p>
